Using a stochastic epidemic model to guide efficient seroprevalence sampling decisions

Poster supplement — data description, ethics statement, and references for Manathunga et al., Adaptive sample size design for serological surveillance during an epidemic using particle filter predictions.

Data

During the COVID-19 serosurveillance program, Canadian Blood Services (CBS) tested residual EDTA plasma retention samples from blood donors using different antibody assays as the pandemic evolved. In the early phase of surveillance (mid-2020), samples were screened for IgG antibodies against the SARS-CoV-2 nucleocapsid (N) protein using the Abbott Architect SARS-CoV-2 IgG chemiluminescent microparticle immunoassay (CMIA) on Abbott Architect immunoassay analyzers, allowing detection of antibodies generated following natural infection.

As vaccination campaigns began in 2021 and the need arose to distinguish infection-induced from vaccine-induced immunity, CBS adopted a dual-assay approach using the Roche Elecsys Anti-SARS-CoV-2 spike semi-quantitative immunoassay (Roche Diagnostics International Ltd., Rotkreuz, Switzerland), measuring total antibodies (IgA, IgM, IgG) to the SARS-CoV-2 S1/receptor-binding domain (anti-S), together with the Roche Elecsys Anti-SARS-CoV-2 qualitative immunoassay measuring total antibodies to the nucleocapsid antigen (anti-N).

We used serological data spanning May 2020 through November 2023, comprising 1,103,191 weekly serological records from 448,241 unique blood donors across 9 Canadian provinces, grouped into four modeled regions. Strata were defined by province, sex, and age group (18–26, 27–36, 37–46, 47–56, and 57+ years), with stratum population sizes drawn from Statistics Canada.

Ethics statement

The SARS-CoV-2 serosurveillance study was approved by the Research Ethics Board of Canadian Blood Services. This secondary analysis was approved by the Faculty of Medicine & Health Sciences Research Ethics Board Office, McGill University, Montreal, Canada (REB number 22-03-077).
